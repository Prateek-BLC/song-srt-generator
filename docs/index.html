How it works

Transcription guesses. This one knows.

Online tools transcribe sung vocals and get them wrong — instruments bleed in, choruses loop, words hallucinate. This app takes the lyrics you already have and force-aligns them to the singing. Every subtitle matches a lyric line exactly as written.

STEP 01

Isolate the vocals

An AI model strips the instrumental away, leaving a clean vocal track — on Apple Silicon it runs on the GPU in seconds. You get the isolated vocal stem as a free bonus download.

STEP 02

Align your lyrics

Every word you pasted is matched to the exact moment it's sung, with word-level timestamps. Your line breaks are respected — one written line becomes one subtitle.

STEP 03

Build the SRT

Single-line cues, capped for your format, no overlaps, imports cleanly into CapCut, Premiere and YouTube. Click any cue in the preview to hear that moment in the song.

Features

Built for people who ship reels, not settings panels.

Made for vertical

Pick 9:16 (Reels / TikTok) or 16:9 (YouTube) and every subtitle stays a single line under the right length cap — 25 characters vertical, 42 horizontal. Never two lines on a phone screen.

Punctuation is the edit

A comma or full stop in your lyric sheet always starts a new subtitle. Punctuate the way the song breathes, and the captions follow the music.

Word-level timing

Cues appear a beat before the vocal lands (karaoke-style), and every split lands on the exact word boundary — not arithmetic.

Private by design

Everything runs on your machine. Songs never leave your computer, nothing is uploaded, there's no account and no telemetry. After setup, it works with Wi-Fi off.

Installs itself

The zip carries its own Python, its own ffmpeg, its own AI models — nothing to pre-install, nothing global touched. Delete the folder and it's fully gone.

Waiting is entertainment

While it works, the status line runs a rotating set of jokes written for editors and AI people — with rare easter eggs for the observant. No two runs repeat.

Dark mode

Studio at night.

One click in the corner. A warm midnight console, not an inverted afterthought.
